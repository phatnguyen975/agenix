# Select the Technology Stack

## Inputs and Outputs

Read the root project instructions and `config.agenix.json` for approved scope, targets and documentation language. Use the merged `docs/PRD.md`, feature catalog and relevant feature records for product outcomes and quality targets. On the architecture branch, verify approval of `docs/blueprint/domain-model.md` and, where system design applies, `docs/blueprint/system-design.md` and its ADRs. Extract owned boundaries, workflows, data semantics, quality drivers and technology constraints. On amendments, also inspect the approved stack, manifests, lockfiles and relevant operating evidence.

Write `docs/blueprint/tech-stack.md` using the [Tech Stack Template](../assets/tech-stack-template.md). Capture approved choices by release unit or shared component and record where each runs. Use the existing architecture amendment route if a choice requires changing an approved boundary, constraint or quality target.

## 1. Derive Technology Needs

For frontend-only scope, first confirm the Client Design from the domain model, PRD and external contracts. Resolve relevant rendering and routing, state ownership, storage, browser security and provider integration before selecting dependent technologies. For other scopes, carry forward the applicable approved system direction. Record client design in the stack document where applicable and reference existing system decisions where they already cover it. Use focused [Evolutionary Architecture Design](../../evolutionary-architecture-design/SKILL.md) for material open design trade-offs.

Identify release units from actual build and release boundaries. Record their roles and targets. A fullstack product can ship client and server together or separately. Record selections for each role even when they share a release unit. Use approved responsibilities and evidence to derive required components. For persistence, examine access patterns, consistency and recovery. For asynchronous or real-time behavior, examine delivery semantics, throughput, latency and replay needs. For caching, establish the measured or expected bottleneck, freshness and invalidation requirements. Include a queue, cache or other service only when the design calls for it. Consider reuse of an existing component when it meets the need.

Reuse mandates and compatible choices already approved in system design or ADRs. Ask early about material gaps in team expertise, maintenance ownership, available training, budget and delivery constraints. Treat team preferences as decision inputs alongside domain fit and distinguish them from explicit mandates.

## 2. Compare and Confirm Choices

1. Group interdependent decisions, such as a language, runtime and framework with compatibility constraints. For each open material choice, including required databases, queues and caches, compare at least two viable candidates when available. If only one passes, show what was considered and the constraint that ruled alternatives out. Carry forward confirmed choices without repeating the comparison.
2. Screen against hard constraints first. Mark each as met, failed or unverified with evidence. A failed constraint eliminates the candidate unless the owning baseline is amended. Resolve unverified constraints that could change the choice before approval. For viable candidates, compare domain and quality fit, ecosystem and support lifecycle, team capability, integration and operational burden, cost, and portability as relevant. Use the same criteria across candidates. For complex decisions, agree the relative importance of criteria and use an evidence-backed ordinal matrix such as Strong, Adequate and Weak, with unknowns shown separately. Explain disagreements between criteria and avoid treating the highest score as approval.
3. Verify material version, support, compatibility and provider claims against official release notes, documentation or measured evidence. State assumptions for estimates. Suggest a focused experiment when evidence cannot settle a material uncertainty. Recommend the best fit under the approved constraints and explain the runner-up's trade-offs. When candidates satisfy the same needs with comparable consequences, account for the team's ability to ship and maintain each choice.
4. Confirm decisions with the human in dependency order. When several consequential choices interact, apply [Grilling](../../grilling/SKILL.md) to ask manageable rounds. Group independent questions and wait for prerequisite answers before asking dependent questions. Present the recommendation, alternatives, trade-offs and evidence gaps. Carry confirmed outcomes into the stack and applicable ADRs. Keep unresolved choices visible in conversation and pause affected work.

## 3. Resolve Hosting and Versions

For each component that runs as a service, select its deployment or consumption model and environment placement. Compare viable hosting options for open material choices using the same confirmation flow. Reassess the technology shortlist if hosting limits invalidate an earlier assumption. Record the engine or protocol separately from the provider product, plan or region where material, operational owner and local, CI, staging or production variants where applicable. For containers, record the image repository and approved tag or digest. For managed services, record the exposed engine version or compatibility level and provider version policy if the provider controls upgrades. Verify meaningful differences in extensions, access, capacity, durability, backups and recovery. Keep credentials and private connection values outside the document.

Record supported versions or explicitly approved version ranges and their compatibility constraints. Identify the pin to apply during scaffold and the lockfile or image reference that will enforce it. Record testing constraints and any testing tools already mandated or approved. The test strategy selects the remaining tools based on verification needs. Scaffold owns executable commands and environment setup.

## 4. Record, Verify and Publish

Apply the [ADR Guide](../../evolutionary-architecture-design/references/adr-guide.md#decide-what-deserves-a-record) to new or materially changed decisions. Use the [ADR Template](../../evolutionary-architecture-design/assets/adr-template.md) under `docs/blueprint/decisions/` when the decision changes an architectural boundary, a major quality or operating commitment, or creates significant switching cost. Reuse and link applicable ADRs. Follow their approval and supersession rules and keep routine tooling details in the stack. Link ADRs from the affected choices.

Check that every selected component traces to a requirement or approved design responsibility. Verify that shared selections and per-unit versions fit together, service placement is explicit, links resolve, and rejected or pending choices have not been recorded as approved. Apply [Artifact Gates](../../../references/workflow-control.md#artifact-gates) to the final document and applicable ADRs. Present the candidate and base, decisions and evidence, outstanding risks and intended publication. Publish approved outputs on the architecture branch and follow the shared phase and session rules.
